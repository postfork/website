Design specification · pre-launch

Bitcoin script with CTV and CSFS, backed only by bitcoin

Postfork is a proposed new blockchain whose consensus is Bitcoin Core with BIP-119 (OP_CHECKTEMPLATEVERIFY) and BIP-348 (OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK) active from block 0. It has no native token, no block subsidy and no premine. Its only asset is pBTC (Postfork): bitcoin pegged in from Bitcoin.

The aim is a place to build and use, with real bitcoin instead of valueless test coins, what these two covenant opcodes would enable on Bitcoin. The peg follows the zkBTC design: a BitVM2 reserve that anyone may register to join as an operator. The current members certify each epoch and can censor admission, so you must verify that you were admitted before the first mint-eligible epoch. Then the only operator you need to trust with your bitcoin can be you, under the assumptions stated on this page, including conditions on block production.

What Postfork is

One chain, one reserve, one asset

Postfork has two halves. The chain: Bitcoin Core consensus with BIP-119 and BIP-348 active from genesis, zero subsidy, and a small, explicitly delimited peg extension. The reserve: a BitVM2 construction on Bitcoin, adapted from zkBTC, that holds the vault BTC intended to back pBTC, subject to the stated assumptions.

Bitcoin as it will look if CTV and CSFS activate — designed to be usable with pegged bitcoin once all launch gates clear, with no new asset, and with a peg in which the only operator you need to trust can be you.

Root principle of the specification. A design goal, not a status: nothing is usable yet.

01Bitcoin Core, plus two BIPs

Script, opcodes and policy are those of a pinned Bitcoin Core release, with BIP-119 and BIP-348 implemented as written and always on. Every other difference is listed in a register of sixteen deltas. Deviations from the BIPs: none.

02Only bitcoin

No subsidy at any height, no premine, no genesis allocation, no developer fund. pBTC is created only when Postfork consensus verifies a Bitcoin vault deposit at deep finality. Redemption requires a burn record (PFB1); like bitcoin, pBTC can also leave circulation through other provably unspendable outputs or fees a block does not claim.

03A peg you can operate

Anyone may register to join the operator set; the current members certify each epoch and can censor admission. The set only grows, and every new vault is pre-signed by all current members. Be admitted before the first mint-eligible epoch, and verify on your own node that you were, and you are a signer of every vault that can ever back a mint.

What Postfork is not

  • BitcoinA separate chain with its own genesis, its own consensus participants and its own risks. pBTC is a claim on BTC held in a BitVM2 reserve; it is not BTC.
  • A Bitcoin fork with your BTC on itNo UTXO snapshot, no airdrop. Postfork starts with an empty UTXO set. You get pBTC only by pegging in.
  • “Future Bitcoin, guaranteed”Bitcoin may activate a different covenant set, different revisions, or nothing. Postfork is Bitcoin Core with BIP-119 and BIP-348 (pinned revisions) active from genesis, not a prediction.
  • A token projectThere is no token to buy. The only asset is pegged BTC.
  • Trustless, unqualified“Effectively trustless” is a defined term with stated assumptions, and the block-production class matters (below).
  • PrivateEvery peg-in, transfer and burn is public, as on Bitcoin.
  • A rollupPostfork has its own consensus. It does not inherit Bitcoin’s ordering unless the Bitcoin-anchored block-production option (H3) is chosen.
  • The pBTC of pTokens/pNetworkUnrelated. See “Is this the old pBTC?”

Why real money

Today, CTV and CSFS run only with coins worth nothing

Bitcoin Script cannot inspect outputs directly and cannot enforce a persistent, keyless output covenant without an ongoing signer; today that typically requires pre-signed transactions plus key deletion. CTV lets an output enforce a transaction template directly.

One concrete proposal, published in 2025 under the name “CTV + CSFS”, consists of exactly two BIPs: BIP-119 and BIP-348. Both are Draft. Reference implementations exist for regression testing (Bitcoin Core pull requests #31989 and #32247, regtest only, open) and on the Bitcoin Inquisition signet. None is active on Bitcoin. Anyone who wants to build with these opcodes today builds with coins that are worth nothing.

Postfork proposes a place where the script layer is exactly the proposed one, where the only money is bitcoin, and where no operator other than yourself has to be trusted with that bitcoin, under the assumptions this site states: 1-of-N setup honesty, at least one honest challenger acting in time, sound cryptography and a sound validity proof, an honest Bitcoin hashrate majority, block producers that include honest Bitcoin headers, and the canonicity assumption of the block-production class in force (under BP-O1V an honest merge-mining majority). Those conditions on block production remain even if you are your own operator.

Same opcodes, same bytes

Contracts for Postfork use the opcodes exactly as the BIPs define them. If, and only if, Bitcoin activates the same texts, they port unchanged.

Real bitcoin, no second asset

Every pBTC is minted only against a vault deposit on the Bitcoin chain that Postfork’s header view shows as best. That is real backing only under the stated assumptions: block producers that censor honest Bitcoin headers (Attack H) or a Bitcoin reorganization of 2016 or more blocks that orphans a settled deposit can leave consensus-valid pBTC unbacked. Fees are paid in pBTC. There is nothing else on the chain.

A peg that needs no soft fork

The reserve uses only constructions proven on Bitcoin mainnet and does not depend on CTV, CSFS or any other inactive soft fork. The peg cannot wait for the soft fork it emulates.

CTV + CSFS

Two opcodes, nothing else

Postfork activates exactly BIP-119 and BIP-348 from block 0, implemented as written. The only change is activation, which the BIPs leave to the deployment.

Together: rebindable signatures

# witness (top last)
<sig> <H>
# tapleaf
OP_CHECKTEMPLATEVERIFY   # H must match the spending tx; H stays on the stack
<P>
OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK     # BIP-340 signature by P over H
Why not OP_INTERNALKEY?

The proposal published as “CTV + CSFS” (open letter; Delving Bitcoin) consists of exactly BIP-119 and BIP-348, and the Bitcoin Core reference pull requests cover exactly those two. BIP-349 (OP_INTERNALKEY) appears only in larger bundles such as LNHANCE and BIP-448. Including it would track a different future.

It adds no spend path that cannot be written for each concrete output: a script can push the key explicitly. The price is about 8 vbytes per rebindable leaf and the loss of dynamic binding to the output’s internal key (a leaf cannot be reused unchanged across outputs with different internal keys, and a rekey does not rebind it automatically). Leaving 0xcb as OP_SUCCESS203 keeps the slot free should Bitcoin activate it; adding it later would, like any rule change the reserve checks, need a new chain or retired vaults. APO, OP_CAT, OP_PAIRCOMMIT, OP_CHECKCONTRACTVERIFY, OP_TEMPLATEHASH and BIP-54 are excluded on the same ground.

Hazards, stated rather than hidden
  • Anyone-can-spend on Bitcoin today. On Bitcoin, where neither opcode is active, a tapscript containing 0xcc is an OP_SUCCESS script and <H> OP_CTV is <H> OP_NOP4. BTC sent on Bitcoin to a Postfork covenant script is lost to the first taker. Wallets must refuse to build such transactions.
  • Amount footgun. CTV does not commit to input amounts. An underfunded single-input template output is unspendable forever; an overfunded one donates the excess to fees (Bitcoin Optech).
  • Cross-chain replay. A CSFS signature over a template hash is valid for any output carrying the same leaf on any chain where the opcodes are active. Never reuse a CSFS key across chains. Postfork does not add a chain identifier to sighashes or templates, because that would break fidelity.

pBTC (Postfork)

The peg: bitcoin in, bitcoin out

pBTC is minted only against a Bitcoin vault deposit that Postfork consensus verifies at deep finality, and redeemed by burning first. The reserve design is zkBTC’s, cited and adapted, not rebranded.

Peg-in

  1. Join (optional, recommended)Register for the operator set before the first mint-eligible epoch, and verify on your own node that you were admitted.
  2. DepositPay exactly one denomination (0.1, 1 or 10 BTC) plus the fixed MoveToBacked fee MTB_FEE plus the total value of the fixed fee-anchor outputs (both pinned at genesis; the fee goes to Bitcoin miners, the anchor outputs are separate outputs of MoveToBacked that allow fee bumping) to a deposit taproot. Any other amount cannot mint. Fix your Postfork claim script first.
  3. Presign and vaultAll operators of the epoch pre-sign the deposit’s BitVM2 graph, including MoveToBacked, which commits your claim script; you co-sign. The vault has a NUMS internal key: no key-path spend, no live signing key.
  4. FinalityWait until MoveToBacked is at least 2016 Bitcoin blocks deep in BtcView and at least the genesis-pinned minimum work W_mint (2016 blocks at the checkpoint’s difficulty) counted from its block to the BtcView tip; BtcView is the Bitcoin header chain that Postfork consensus tracks. Normally about two weeks; longer if Bitcoin’s difficulty is below the checkpoint’s.
  5. MintSubmit the mint transaction. Every Postfork full node checks the proof (clauses M1–M11). pBTC appear in the mint transaction’s outputs that your claim script (the mint authorization script) authorizes, minus the fee.

If MoveToBacked does not confirm, the depositor can refund after a timelock; Script sets no deadline for MoveToBacked, so after the timelock the two race, which is why the depositor signs it last, broadcasts it at once and bumps its fee through the anchors if needed. Once MoveToBacked fires without a later mint, the BTC is an irrevocable, consented contribution to the reserve.

Peg-out (burn first)

  1. BurnPublish a burn of an available vault denomination, naming your Bitcoin key and a maximum fee. Use a key your wallet controls: a well-formed key nobody holds loses the payout. There is no un-burn.
  2. FrontAfter the burn is final on Postfork, an admitted operator whose identity belongs to the epoch set of the vault it will claim from, and whose bond for that epoch is live and sequencing connector valid, pays you on Bitcoin from its own funds. That can be you, under the same conditions: verified admission alone is not enough, and a slash, or the return of your bond for that epoch, ends the capability.
  3. ClaimThe operator claims reimbursement along the pre-signed graph.
  4. ChallengeDuring the window, anyone running a Bitcoin and a Postfork full node may disprove a false claim. A successful disprove slashes the operator’s bond and pays the challenger a bounty on Bitcoin.
  5. ReimbursementWithout a successful disprove, the operator receives exactly the burned amount from one vault.

Burn-first has a cost: a burn that no operator serves, for example because the maximum fee was set too low, stays burned with no payout until someone serves it.

Effectively trustless: a defined term

You do not trust other operators with your bitcoin. You are an operator. You run your own nodes. It does not mean “zero assumptions”, and it does not mean “trust a company, a federation or a gatekeeper”. It also does not make block producers irrelevant: safety assumes that they include honest Bitcoin headers and that the canonicity assumption of the block-production class in force holds.

  1. Join the operator set. Anyone may register to join; the current members certify each epoch and can censor admission. After admission nobody leaves and nobody is removed. Every new vault is pre-signed by the current full set.
  2. Join before the first mint. If you, or a party you treat as honest, are in the set before the first mint-eligible epoch, every vault that can ever back a mint includes you, and you do not sign a malicious graph. That closes Attack B, but only if you were actually admitted; under genesis capture with your admission censored, Attack B is fully open for you. Registering is not being admitted: the current members certify each new epoch and can censor joiners, so verify on your own node that you are in the set of the first mint-eligible epoch.
  3. Run your own nodes. A Postfork full node and a Bitcoin full node. Your wallet trusts your nodes.
  4. Exit yourself. Once admitted, you may front and reclaim your own peg-out from a vault whose epoch set includes you, while your bond for that epoch is live and your sequencing connector is valid. A fraud or grief slash ends that capability even though you stay in the set; the return of your bond for an epoch ends it for that epoch’s vaults.
  5. Operators cannot steal under: 1-of-N setup honesty; at least one honest live challenger acting within every relevant window; sound circuit, graph and validity-proof cryptography; a Bitcoin adversary below about 50% of hashrate (inherited from Bitcoin); and the Postfork-side conditions of the block-production class in force: block producers include honest Bitcoin headers (otherwise consensus-valid but unbacked pBTC can be minted and its burns reimbursed, Attack H), and the class’s canonicity assumption holds (under BP-O1V an honest merge-mining majority); and no Bitcoin reorganization of 2016 or more blocks orphans a settled deposit, which would leave minted pBTC unbacked while its burns are reimbursed; and no Postfork reorganization orphans an already reimbursed burn (its pBTC would return while the vault BTC is gone). Worst case under all of those assumptions: freeze, not theft. Outside them (Attack H, F, V, a reorganization that orphans a settled deposit, or one that orphans an already reimbursed burn), unbacked pBTC can be reimbursed from the reserve.
  6. What this does not close. Holders who never register depend on at least one live admitted operator with a live bond and valid sequencing connector for a matching vault’s epoch. Being your own operator does not protect against block producers that censor honest headers, or against a failed canonicity assumption of the class. A censoring block-production majority can delay burns. A frozen coin is not made recoverable.

The claim may be made only for a deployment that has cleared every launch gate, runs a permitted block-production class and has the Postfork validity proof. Today none of that holds.

Prominent claim, verbatim from the specification (§11.3): pBTC is effectively trustless only in the self-operator sense of §1.1.1, only for the block-production class in force and while its assumptions hold, including that block producers include honest Bitcoin headers, and only after all gates clear. Under the stated assumptions, including that no Bitcoin reorganization orphans a settled deposit, no Postfork reorganization orphans an already reimbursed burn, and no Attack H, F or V succeeds, the worst case is freeze, not theft, and a freeze may be permanent.

When is pBTC safe

pBTC is safe for you if you (or an operator you trust) were admitted to the operator set of the first mint-eligible epoch, as verified on your own node, and refuse malicious graphs, at least one honest challenger watches both chains and acts in time, the cryptography and the Postfork validity proof are sound, Bitcoin's hashrate majority is honest and no Bitcoin reorganization orphans a settled deposit, Postfork block producers include honest Bitcoin headers, and the block-production class in force is one that §11.2 permits for pBTC and its canonicity assumption holds (under BP-O1V: an honest merge-mining majority) and no Postfork reorganization orphans an already reimbursed burn — and even then, the worst case is a freeze, which is not theft but may never be recoverable.

Verbatim from the specification, §9.3. §11.2 lists the permitted block-production classes.

Freeze is not theft; “not theft” is not “recoverable”

Under the stated assumptions, including that no Bitcoin reorganization orphans a settled deposit, no Postfork reorganization orphans an already reimbursed burn, and no Attack H, F or V succeeds, the worst case of a dishonest operator, a stuck graph or a vanished operator set is that vaults or burned coins freeze. No one gains them. That is not theft. It is also not recovery: there is no council, no admin key and no rescue path. A frozen vault may stay frozen forever.

A soundness bug in the BitVM2 graph, the reimbursement statement or the validity proof can enable theft, as can a consensus bug shared by nodes and the validity-proof specification.

The specification lists twenty-two residual assumptions, including the premise risk that Bitcoin may activate a different covenant set or nothing.

Optional gatekeeper

A genesis-pinned key that can only refuse new mints, for source-of-funds screening. No freeze list, no clawback, no role in transfers, burns, payouts or challenges. The product default is none. If set, it censors all new peg-ins of the chain, and it is never what makes pBTC safe.

One more limit than zkBTC

zkBTC follows one light client, Bitcoin. Postfork has its own consensus, so its peg-out verifier must follow a Bitcoin and a Postfork light client. The peg can be no stronger than the weaker of the Bitcoin-side reserve and what the BitVM2 verifier can establish about Postfork (BitVM2 bridge paper).

Use cases

What the opcodes enable

After the mint, pBTC are ordinary Postfork outputs. Any script valid under Bitcoin Core with BIP-119 and BIP-348 works, and nothing in the peg constrains transfers. The specification names these uses; it does not ship applications.

Payment trees

One output commits, through CTV templates, to a tree of transactions that pays many recipients later.

CTV · source: BIP-119

Vaults

Coins that can only move along pre-committed transactions, for example a withdrawal path with a delay.

CTV (CSFS optional) · source: BIP-119

Rebindable signatures

A key signs a template hash instead of a transaction, and can sign a new template later for any coin carrying the leaf.

CTV + CSFS · source: BIP-348

LN-Symmetry-style channels

Payment channels built on rebindable signatures.

CTV + CSFS · sources: BIP-348, Optech

Ark-style timeout trees

Shared outputs that unroll into individual outputs along committed templates.

CTV (CSFS optional) · source: Delving Bitcoin

DLCs

Contracts that settle against an oracle’s BIP-340 attestation, checked in script.

CSFS; CTV optional for templated settlement · source: Optech

Delegation

A key holder authorizes a spend by signing a message that the script verifies.

CSFS · source: BIP-348

Mint toward a covenant

A claim script cannot be a bare CTV template: the mint carries its own proof as an output, so the template would have to commit to itself. A claim leaf that checks a CTV hash signed with CSFS after the proof is known can still send pBTC into a covenant; otherwise mint to a key and spend into the covenant.

clause M10

Every use carries the script hazards above. Fees on templates must be pre-committed, paid by an anchor output, or paid by committed extra inputs.

Principles

What the design refuses to trade away

Each principle is a numbered requirement of the specification. Where a decision does not follow from the root principle, it is labelled as a trade-off.

No token, no premine

Block subsidy zero at every height. No premine, genesis allocation, developer fund or founder reward. Block producers earn pBTC fees only.

REQ-2

No admin keys, no council

No emergency key, alert key, hardcoded Postfork block checkpoint, checkpoint key or governance function, in any edition. (The genesis-pinned Bitcoin header that roots BtcView is a reproducible consensus parameter, not such a checkpoint.) The design refuses the trust root of a security council and pays for it with irreversibility.

REQ-6

Core fidelity

Every difference to the pinned Bitcoin Core release is in the delta register. An implementation that differs anywhere else does not conform.

REQ-1

Self-operator trust model

You are an operator, you run your own nodes, and you trust no other operator with your bitcoin, but only under every condition listed in the full definition, and only after all gates clear.

§1.1.1

Mainnet-proven dependencies

The reserve uses only constructions proven on Bitcoin mainnet. Anything that is not mainnet-proven is a named, blocking launch gate.

REQ-7

Exit without permission

Every holder can publish a burn without any gatekeeper; no gatekeeper step exists in the peg-out. A BTC payout is not guaranteed: it needs the burn included and final on Postfork, a matching unspent vault, a maximum fee that clears an operator’s costs, and at least one admitted, live, liquid and willing operator of that vault’s epoch. Anyone, including the holder, may request admission as an operator; the current members certify admission and can censor it. Serving your own exit requires verified admission and a live bond and valid sequencing connector for the vault’s epoch (a slash ends it); otherwise exit depends on at least one admitted, live operator.

Open questions

Who produces blocks? Not decided

How Postfork blocks are produced is an open decision (gate G5). No option is adopted. It matters because the peg can be no stronger than what its verifier can establish about Postfork, and whether a burn is canonical depends on the block-production rule. Mints depend on it too: producers who censor honest Bitcoin headers can, with a private Bitcoin branch at least 2016 blocks deep that carries at least the fixed minimum work W_mint (2016 blocks at the checkpoint’s difficulty; Bitcoin’s timewarp weakness would let such a branch reach that depth with less work, which is why the work floor exists, and once Bitcoin’s difficulty has risen above the checkpoint’s the fixed floor equals fewer than 2016 current blocks; one such branch can carry many mints) from a minority miner, make a mint against a non-canonical Bitcoin branch valid (Attack H).

Non-binding recommendation

H3 for fork choice, so that burn canonicity reduces to Bitcoin. O1 merge mining as the default way blocks are proposed. Optional pre-confirmations with no peg role. The Postfork validity proof as a blocking gate in every case. Reject O2 for a real-money peg; reject O3 as a safety source.

This records the direction the research supports. It decides nothing. A fallback to O1 alone must state that burn canonicity rests on the merge-mining majority.

Merge mining does not inherit Bitcoin’s security

Joining costs a Bitcoin miner almost nothing, and for the same reason an attack by a large pool costs it no Bitcoin revenue. Merge-mined chains have operated with single pools holding majorities for long periods (“Merged Mining: Curse or Cure?”). Fee-only mining is not security-equivalent to subsidized mining (CCS 2016).

Other open questions

  • Validity proof: which proof system proves Postfork block validity inside BitVM2, and whether proving fits inside the challenge window (gate G4).
  • Data availability: how Bitcoin-side challengers obtain Postfork data in time; under H3, what happens to an anchored block whose data is withheld, and who pays for anchors.
  • Genesis capture: a party that registers the first epoch could refuse to certify later ones and freeze peg-ins, or certify them with its own Sybil identities only while censoring honest joiners and then mint against Sybil-only vaults. The registration-only epochs do not prevent this. Until a mitigation is specified and adversarially reviewed, users must verify that they, or an operator they trust, are in the set of the first mint-eligible epoch.
  • Chain identifiers: registering the address prefixes and coin type, and re-pinning the BIP revisions and the Bitcoin Core base release at launch.

Status

Pre-launch. Every gate except G1 open

Postfork is a design specification. There is no code, no running chain and no pBTC. Both gate families below, the four requirement gates and the maturity gates G1–G6, must clear before anything may be called pBTC or described as launched. Specifying a rule, an encoding or a number does not clear a gate. There are no dates. The lists below give every acceptance criterion of the specification (§12) in short form; the specification’s wording governs.

Any BTC-backed asset on a Postfork-rules chain that does not meet the full specification, such as a custodial or federated bridge, a deployment without the validity proof, or a test deployment, must use a different name and must not promise migration into pBTC. Test deployments may use “tpBTC (Postfork test)” for valueless test coins only.
