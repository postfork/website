Can I use it today?

No.

Postfork is a pre-launch design specification. There is no code, no running chain, no pBTC (Postfork) and no test network. Two families of launch gates must both clear: the four requirement gates (growth-only operator set encoded, setup integrity, holder-to-operator onboarding, challenger economics) and the maturity gates G2–G6, including the block-production decision. All of them are open; only G1 (BitVM2 mainnet-proven) has cleared. Nothing on this site describes a live system.

What is Postfork?

A proposed new blockchain from block 0 whose consensus is Bitcoin Core with BIP-119 (OP_CHECKTEMPLATEVERIFY) and BIP-348 (OP_CHECKSIGFROMSTACK) active from genesis, plus a small, explicitly delimited peg extension. It has no native token, no block subsidy and no premine. Its only asset is pBTC (Postfork): bitcoin pegged in from Bitcoin through a BitVM2 reserve adapted from zkBTC.

The purpose is to build and use, with real bitcoin instead of valueless test coins, what CTV and CSFS would enable on Bitcoin.

Why a new chain?

Neither opcode is active on Bitcoin; both BIPs are Draft (BIP-119, BIP-348). Today they run in regression tests and on the Bitcoin Inquisition signet, with coins worth nothing. Postfork is not a soft-fork proposal and takes no position on whether or how Bitcoin should activate anything. It offers a separate place where the script layer is exactly the proposed one and the only money is bitcoin.

Is Postfork a fork of Bitcoin? Do I get coins?

No, and no. “Post-fork” refers to Bitcoin after the CTV + CSFS soft fork. Postfork does not copy Bitcoin’s chain history: it starts from its own genesis with an empty UTXO set. There is no snapshot and no airdrop. You get pBTC only by pegging in BTC.

What changes compared to Bitcoin Core?

Sixteen listed deltas and nothing else. They fall into four groups:

  • Identity: own genesis, network magic, ports, address prefix pf; no hardcoded checkpoints.
  • Money: block subsidy zero at every height.
  • Script: BIP-119 and BIP-348 active from genesis, as written. Deviations from the BIPs: none.
  • Peg: Bitcoin header tracking (BtcView), operator-epoch registration, the peg-in mint rule and the burn rule, carried in tagged OP_RETURN outputs with no serialization change.

Block production (D-13) is the open part. Serialization, signature hashes, Taproot and block weight stay as in Bitcoin Core, and so do ordinary fee, RBF, package and TRUC policy (in pBTC). CTV/CSFS standardness and the relay of peg transactions follow the listed policy deltas D-06, D-15 and D-16. See the delta table.

Why is OP_INTERNALKEY not included?

Because the proposal published as “CTV + CSFS” (open letter; Bitcoin Optech) contains exactly BIP-119 and BIP-348, and the Bitcoin Core reference pull requests (#31989, #32247) cover exactly those two. BIP-349 appears only in larger bundles (LNHANCE, BIP-448). It adds no capability, since a script can push the key explicitly for about 8 vbytes. 0xcb stays OP_SUCCESS203, a free slot should Bitcoin ever activate it.

Is pBTC (Postfork) the old pTokens pBTC?

No. pBTC (Postfork) has no relation to that token, its issuers, or its holders.

An unrelated token with the ticker pBTC exists: the pTokens wrapped bitcoin of the pNetwork project. Its BSC variant was exploited on 2021-09-19; according to the project’s post-mortem, the attacker took 277 BTC, most of that bridge’s collateral (coverage: The Block). The project later announced the end of life of its v2 bridges (pNetwork on X, secondary source).

To avoid confusion, Postfork writes “pBTC (Postfork)” at first mention, and wallets must identify it by chain (the Postfork genesis hash), never by ticker alone.

In what precise sense is it effectively trustless?

Effectively trustless, in a defined sense, and only after all gates clear.

You do not trust other operators with your bitcoin. You are an operator. You run your own nodes. Concretely: anyone may register to join the operator set; the current members certify each epoch and can censor admission; the set only grows and nobody is removed after admission; every new vault is pre-signed by the whole current set. If you, or a party you treat as honest, are admitted before the first mint-eligible epoch, every vault that can ever back a mint includes you, and you will not sign a graph that lets anyone steal. You run a Postfork and a Bitcoin full node, and, once your admission is verified, you can serve your own exit. Registering is not being admitted: the current members certify each epoch and can censor joiners, so you must check on your own node that you are in the set of the first mint-eligible epoch.

It does not mean zero assumptions. Operators cannot steal only under 1-of-N setup honesty, at least one honest live challenger acting in every window, sound circuit, graph and validity-proof cryptography, a Bitcoin adversary below about 50% of hashrate (inherited from Bitcoin), and the Postfork-side conditions of the block-production class in force: block producers include honest Bitcoin headers (otherwise consensus-valid but unbacked pBTC can be minted and its burns reimbursed, Attack H), and the class’s canonicity assumption holds (under BP-O1V an honest merge-mining majority). Being your own operator does not protect against either failing. Attack B is closed for you only if you were actually admitted; under genesis capture with your admission censored it is fully open. Holders who never register still depend on one live operator, and censoring block producers can delay burns.

The specification states it in one sentence:

pBTC is safe for you if you (or an operator you trust) were admitted to the operator set of the first mint-eligible epoch, as verified on your own node, and refuse malicious graphs, at least one honest challenger watches both chains and acts in time, the cryptography and the Postfork validity proof are sound, Bitcoin's hashrate majority is honest, Postfork block producers include honest Bitcoin headers, and the block-production class in force is one that §11.2 permits for pBTC and its canonicity assumption holds (under BP-O1V: an honest merge-mining majority) — and even then, the worst case is a freeze, which is not theft but may never be recoverable.

Its prominent claim (§11.3), verbatim:

pBTC is effectively trustless only in the self-operator sense of §1.1.1, only for the block-production class in force and while its assumptions hold, including that block producers include honest Bitcoin headers, and only after all gates clear. The worst case under the stated assumptions is freeze, not theft, and a freeze may be permanent.

§11.2 of the specification lists the permitted block-production classes. Today no class has been chosen.

Can operators steal the bitcoin?

Not under all of the stated assumptions: 1-of-N setup honesty, at least one honest live challenger acting within every window, sound circuit, graph and validity-proof cryptography, a Bitcoin adversary below about 50% of hashrate, block producers that include honest Bitcoin headers (otherwise consensus-valid but unbacked pBTC can be minted and its burns reimbursed from the reserve, Attack H), and the canonicity assumption of the block-production class in force (under BP-O1V an honest merge-mining majority; otherwise a burn on a non-canonical Postfork fork can be reimbursed, Attack F). The vault has a NUMS internal key and no live signing key; BTC moves only along the pre-signed BitVM2 graph, and every reimbursement claim can be disproved by anyone.

Freeze is not theft; “not theft” is not “recoverable”. Only if all of these assumptions hold is the worst case that vaults or burned coins freeze. No one gains them, but there is no council, no admin key and no rescue path, so a freeze may be permanent. A soundness bug in the graph, the reimbursement statement or the validity proof can enable theft, as can a consensus bug shared by nodes and the validity-proof specification.

Who produces blocks?

Not decided. Block production is an open decision (gate G5).

The options are merge mining (O1), an own SHA-256 proof of work (O2), a federated signing quorum (O3), and hybrids: O1 with soft pre-confirmations (H1), O3 with a validity proof (H2), and Bitcoin-anchored fork choice (H3). The choice matters for the peg: the BitVM2 verifier must establish that a burn is on the canonical Postfork chain, and how strong that is depends on the rule. It matters for mints too: producers who keep honest Bitcoin headers out of Postfork blocks can, together with a private branch of 2016 Bitcoin-difficulty blocks, make a mint against a non-canonical Bitcoin branch valid (Attack H). That needs no Bitcoin majority, only a miner willing to spend that work, so mint canonicity also depends on block production.

The specification’s non-binding recommendation is H3 for fork choice, so that burn canonicity reduces to Bitcoin, with merge mining as the default way blocks are proposed and the Postfork validity proof required in every case. It rejects O2 for a real-money peg and O3 as a safety source. Merge mining alone does not inherit Bitcoin’s security (ePrint 2017/791). Block producers earn pBTC fees only; there is no subsidy.

What happens when Bitcoin activates CTV and CSFS?

Postfork does not follow Bitcoin automatically, and no edition may change the meaning of an opcode in existing scripts. The specification covers each case:

  • Bitcoin activates BIP-119 and BIP-348 with identical semantics: Postfork has converged and its consensus is unchanged. The cross-chain replay hazard of rebindable signatures becomes live, and wallets must warn. CTV and CSFS can at most replace parts of the peg’s pre-signed graph with templates, as a new vault generation under adversarial review. They cannot verify a Postfork burn proof in script, so the dependency on a live operator for exits stays; an exit anyone can satisfy would need more capability, such as OP_CAT-class introspection.
  • Same opcodes, different revisions: Postfork keeps the pinned semantics.
  • A different bundle, for example BIP-448: Postfork may add those opcodes in their Bitcoin slots, which it kept free, but never removes CTV. It is then a superset.
  • CTV only: CSFS remains a Postfork extension.
  • Nothing: the premise stays hypothetical and is described as such.

Existing vaults are never changed in place. Holders move by burning and re-pegging. A loosening change, or a change to the header format, fork choice or block production, is a hard-fork edition: existing vaults redeem only burns that are valid under the rules their graphs were signed for. Such an edition must therefore be a new chain with burn-and-re-peg migration, or must not be adopted while existing vaults back circulating pBTC.

How would I get pBTC?

By pegging in, once everything is live. You pay one denomination (0.1, 1 or 10 BTC; provisional) into a deposit taproot. The operator set pre-signs the BitVM2 graph, including a transaction called MoveToBacked that moves the deposit into a vault and commits your Postfork claim script; you co-sign. When MoveToBacked is 2016 Bitcoin blocks deep, about two weeks, you submit a mint transaction. Every Postfork full node checks the proof against the Bitcoin headers it tracks, and pBTC appear at your claim script.

Joining the operator set before the first mint-eligible epoch is optional and recommended: once your admission is verified on your own node, it makes you the only operator you need to trust. It does not remove the conditions on block production stated above.

How would I get BTC back?

Burn first. You publish a burn of an amount that matches an available vault, naming your Bitcoin key and a maximum fee. Any admitted operator, including you if your admission was verified, pays you on Bitcoin from its own funds and then claims reimbursement from a vault. Anyone may disprove a false claim during the challenge window. No gatekeeper is involved.

The burn is final before any operator acts. If no operator serves it, for example because the maximum fee was too low, the coin stays burned with no payout until someone does. There is no un-burn.

What does it cost to be an operator?

A bond on Bitcoin (2 BTC class; provisional), locked until no vault of that epoch has a remaining claim path, plus participation in the presigning ceremony and fees. Operators front peg-outs from their own BTC and are reimbursed after the challenge window. It is a bonded commitment, not a retail click. A fraudulent claim that is disproved slashes the bond and pays the challenger a bounty on Bitcoin.

Is there a token? Who pays block producers?

There is no token. Fees are paid in pBTC, and block producers earn fees only. Before the first mint no pBTC exists, so no fees exist; the first mint pays its fee out of the minted value. A fee-only security budget is a stated residual, not a feature.

What is the gatekeeper?

An optional key, pinned in the genesis block, that can only refuse new mints, for example for source-of-funds screening. The product default is none. It cannot freeze, claw back or redirect coins, and it has no role in transfers, burns, payouts or challenges. If set, it censors all new peg-ins of the chain. It is never what makes pBTC safe.

Why no admin keys? What happens if there is a bug?

Any admin key, council or emergency key would be a trust root that is not you. So there is none, in any edition. The price is irreversibility: a non-soundness bug can freeze affected vaults for good; a soundness bug can enable theft; nobody can sweep funds to safety. Upgrades are new editions that users choose to run, and vaults migrate only by burn and re-peg. This is why the launch gates require independent re-implementation of the peg rules, differential testing and conformance before anything ships.

Is it private?

No. Postfork is as transparent as Bitcoin and claims no privacy. Peg-ins, transfers, burns, payouts and operator registrations are public, and the peg links a Bitcoin deposit to its Postfork recipient and a burn to its payout. Use fresh keys per deposit, claim script and recipient.

What are the main risks?

Besides the assumptions above: the Postfork validity proof is not mainnet-proven (gate G4); burn canonicity is only as strong as the block-production class (Attack F); mint canonicity depends on block producers not censoring honest Bitcoin headers (Attack H); a deep Bitcoin reorganization beyond 2016 blocks would leave minted pBTC unbacked; wallet mistakes such as sending BTC on Bitcoin to a Postfork covenant script lose funds; and Bitcoin may activate a different covenant set or nothing. The specification lists twenty-two residual assumptions and does not call any of them solved.

Where is the specification?

The specification (edition spec-v1.0, draft) is the single normative document of the project. Its publication in a public repository under github.com/postfork is planned. The whitepaper, this FAQ and the website are informative; where they simplify, the specification governs. No private channel for security reports is open yet: GitHub private vulnerability reporting is not enabled on the repository of this website, and no e-mail address is published. Do not open a public issue for an unpatched soundness flaw; keep the report until a private channel is announced in this answer and in the website repository’s SECURITY.md.
